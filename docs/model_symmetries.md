# Symmetry Notes For Demo Hamiltonians

This note summarizes practical symmetry structure for the demo Hamiltonians in
this repository and clarifies what is broken by disorder terms used for ETH
numerics.

Conventions:

- Spin-1/2 Pauli operators are denoted by $X_i, Y_i, Z_i$.
- "Translation" means one-site lattice translation.
- Statements below assume generic couplings unless an explicit special limit is
  mentioned.


Reading guide:

- "Generic" means parameters are not fine-tuned.
- "Special limit" means symmetry-enhanced parameter choices.

## 1. Transverse-Field Ising Chain

Model:

$$
H_{\mathrm{TFIM}} = -j\sum_i Z_i Z_{i+1} - h\sum_i X_i - g\sum_i Z_i
$$

Typical symmetry content:

- For $g = 0$: global spin-flip (parity) generated by
  $U_X = \prod_i X_i$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry.
- For $g \neq 0$: that $\mathbb{Z}_2$ is explicitly broken.
- For uniform couplings and periodic boundary: translation symmetry is present.
- Open boundaries break lattice translation.

Special limits worth noting:

- If $h=0$ and $g=0$, the model is classical Ising in the $Z$ basis and gains
  additional conserved structure.
- Uniform periodic chains also have lattice reflection symmetry.

ETH relevance:

- Turning on $g$ and/or using open boundaries helps avoid extra block
  structures, reducing accidental degeneracies.

## 2. XXZ Chain

Model:

$$
H_{\mathrm{XXZ}} = j_{xy}\sum_i (X_iX_{i+1}+Y_iY_{i+1}) + j_z\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+1}
$$

Typical symmetry content:

- Conserves total $S^z$ (global U(1) generated by $\sum_i Z_i$).
- Uniform periodic chain has translation symmetry.
- In the isotropic limit ($j_{xy}=j_z$), symmetry is enhanced to SU(2).

Special limits worth noting:

- Away from the isotropic point, the continuous symmetry is U(1) (not SU(2)).
- Uniform periodic chains also have lattice reflection symmetry.

ETH relevance:

- Symmetry sector resolution can be important for finite-size ETH diagnostics.
- SU(2)-enhanced points have larger multiplets and stronger finite-size effects.

## 3. Disordered Heisenberg Chain

Model:

$$
H_{\mathrm{dis.Heis}}
= \sum_i (J_i^x X_iX_{i+1}+J_i^y Y_iY_{i+1}+J_i^z Z_iZ_{i+1})
+ \sum_i (h_i^x X_i + h_i^y Y_i + h_i^z Z_i)
$$

Typical symmetry content:

- Generic independent disorder in couplings/fields breaks translation.
- Generic $(h_i^x,h_i^y,h_i^z)$ also breaks continuous spin-rotation symmetry.
- Generic couplings and fields do not enforce a global spin symmetry;
  additional discrete symmetries may occur for special parameter choices.

Special limits worth noting:

- If onsite fields are all zero, the Hamiltonian is invariant under global
  $\pi$ rotations around spin axes:
  $U_X=\prod_i X_i$, $U_Y=\prod_i Y_i$, $U_Z=\prod_i Z_i$ (Klein-type
  $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ structure modulo phase).
- If only $h_i^z$ is present ($h_i^x=h_i^y=0$), $U_Z$ remains an exact
  symmetry.
- If only $h_i^x$ is present, $U_X$ remains exact; similarly for only $h_i^y$.

ETH relevance:

- This is a common non-integrable setting for robust ETH numerics.
- Weak disorder is used to suppress accidental degeneracies while preserving
  local few-body structure.

## 4. Random Pauli-String Hamiltonian

Model:

$$
H_{\mathrm{rand}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{terms}}} c_k P_k
$$

where each $P_k$ is a sampled local Pauli string.

Typical symmetry content:

- No global spin symmetry is imposed by the constructor; the actual symmetry
  content depends on the sampled terms.
- Because terms are sampled on an open chain, translation is absent by
  construction.

Special limits worth noting:

- Specific random seeds can accidentally produce extra symmetries, but this is
  non-generic and not structurally protected by the constructor.

ETH relevance:

- This model is designed as a generic non-integrable stress test.

## 5. $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ Projective XYZ Chain (Eq. 5.14)

Paper-inspired model:

$$
H_{N=2} = \sum_j\left(J_j^x X_jX_{j+1} + J_j^y Y_jY_{j+1} + J_j^z Z_jZ_{j+1}\right)
+ \alpha\sum_j X_jY_{j+1}Z_{j+2}
$$

with periodic identification in the paper setting.

Symmetry points to keep in mind:

- The first part is an XYZ chain with site-dependent couplings.
- Two key global generators are
  $$
  U_x := \prod_{j=1}^{L} X_j, \quad U_z := \prod_{j=1}^{L} Z_j.
  $$
  Both commute with $H_{N=2}$, including the $\alpha\sum_j X_jY_{j+1}Z_{j+2}$
  term.
- They satisfy
  $$
  U_z U_x = (-1)^L U_x U_z.
  $$
  Hence:
  - even $L$: they commute (ordinary linear representation);
  - odd $L$: they anticommute (nontrivial projective relation for
    $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$).
- The added three-body term proportional to $\alpha$ is used to break an extra
  discrete symmetry (notably one that would force sign flips on some operators,
  such as $Y_j \mapsto -Y_j$).
- In the paper context, this is paired with weak randomness in
  $(J_j^x,J_j^y,J_j^z)$ to remove unwanted spacetime symmetries while keeping
  the target projective symmetry structure.
- At $\alpha=0$, the model reduces to an XYZ Heisenberg-type chain.

Special limits worth noting:

- The minimal projective phase discussed in arXiv:2509.01931 is realized by
  taking odd chain length for $N=2$ (equivalently $L \equiv 1 \pmod 2$).
- For even $L$, the same Hamiltonian still has $U_x,U_z$ symmetries but without
  the projective anticommutation.

ETH relevance:

- This is the direct $N=2$ spin-chain benchmark used in the prETH discussion
  around Eq. (5.14) in arXiv:2509.01931v2.

## Practical Note For This Repository

These symmetry summaries are intentionally concise and operational. For
numerical ETH checks at finite size, weak disorder and mild symmetry-breaking
terms are often introduced to reduce accidental degeneracies and expose
typical-state behavior.
